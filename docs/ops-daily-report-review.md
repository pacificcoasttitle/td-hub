## 0. Read this first — two corrections to what we believed

**A. The email does not arrive on failed days.** I cross-referenced every job run against every email actually sent. The correlation is exact:

| Day | Job status | Report email sent |
|---|---|---|
| Jul 31 | completed | 1 |
| Jul 30 | completed | 1 |
| Jul 29 | **failed** | **0** |
| Jul 28 | **failed** | **0** |
| Jul 27 | completed | 1 |
| Jul 26 | **failed** | **0** |
| Jul 25 | completed | 1 |
| Jul 24 | completed | 1 |
| Jul 23 | **failed** | **0** |
| Jul 22 | **failed** | **0** |
| Jul 21 | **failed** | **0** |
| Jul 20 | completed | 1 |

So on a failed day the owner gets *nothing* — not a truncated or broken email. Part of "I don't know what I'm looking at" may simply be that the report shows up erratically, and an every-other-day email is one you never learn to read.

**B. The "~50% failure rate" is a recent regression, not the long-run norm.** All-time: **59 succeeded / 7 failed (89% success)**. But **6 of those 7 failures happened between Jul 21 and Jul 29**. Before Jul 21 the job was essentially clean; the recent window really is ~50%.

Also worth knowing: **successful runs take ~1 second** (avg 0.8s, max 2s across 59 runs). The report is not slow. The 800–900s durations on failed runs are not runtime — they are the gap until the watchdog reaps a row still marked `running`. So this is not a timeout from the report doing too much work; something makes the invocation die before it can write its result or send its email. The Jul 30/31 runs both succeeded, so the RLS lockdown did not cause it.

**This is a separate bug from the readability work.** Rewriting the content will not fix delivery. I'd treat "why did it start failing on Jul 21" as its own small investigation.

## 2. Readability flags — blunt

**The headline is actually fine.** `🟢 Healthy` + an ATTENTION REQUIRED list is exactly right. It's then buried under fourteen sections of engineering telemetry, so it doesn't read as the answer — it reads as a preamble.

**Section 13 is empty theatre.** Every one of its four rows says "Not tracked". A section that reports nothing, every day, trains the reader to skip — and once you skip section 13 you skip section 12 too.

**Section 7 is three-quarters empty** for the same reason (only "New users created" is real).

**Jargon a non-technical reader cannot parse** — this is the bulk of the email:

- *"FK-only stuck"* — foreign-key-only. Meaningless outside the codebase.
- *"Zero-party orders" / "Zero-party unconfirmed" / "Empty-confirmed orders"* — three near-identical phrases describing different internal states.
- *"Pending enrichment within cooldown"* — "enrichment" and "cooldown" are both internal concepts.
- *"Stuck (terminal)"* — sounds alarming; actually means "stuck in a final state, no further action possible."
- *"Multi-role expansions"*, *"stub companies"*, *"orphan contacts"* — internal data-hygiene vocabulary.
- *"Last completed enrich_orders — 14 min ago"* — a raw job name printed to a human.
- *"Sections loaded: 14/14"* — a health metric **of the report itself**, in the summary line, competing with the business numbers.
- *"Approx. Claude API cost"* — an AI vendor bill in a daily ops email.
- **Section 2 prints raw job names** — `softpro.sync_contacts.selling_agent_broker`, `titlepoint.drain`, `notifications.process_outbox` — with average durations in seconds. This is a table for whoever maintains the cron system, not the product owner.
- **Baselines 17 and 16 are hardcoded constants** (`ZERO_PARTY_UNCONFIRMED_BASELINE`, `FK_ONLY_STUCK_BASELINE`). Alerts fire relative to numbers nobody remembers choosing, and nothing in the email says where they came from.

**The window is confusing.** "Window Jul 30 8:00 AM to Jul 31 8:00 AM" — a rolling 24h that isn't a calendar day, so "yesterday" in the report is not "yesterday" in conversation.

**Verdict:** it reads as a diagnostic dump written by the system for the system. The one line the owner actually needs is the status emoji; everything after it asks him to interpret internal state.

## 5. Proposed plain-English redesign (outline only)

**Design rule:** the owner should get the answer in the first two lines and be able to stop. Everything else is optional depth. Nothing appears unless it's either good news worth confirming or a problem worth acting on.

**Subject line carries the verdict** — so it's readable from a phone notification:
- `TD Hub — all clear (Thursday, July 31)`
- `TD Hub — 2 things need attention (Thursday, July 31)`

### Section A — The headline (always)
> ## ✅ Everything ran clean yesterday
> *Thursday, July 31 · covering 8am Wednesday to 8am Thursday*

or

> ## ⚠️ 2 things need attention
> *Thursday, July 31 · covering 8am Wednesday to 8am Thursday*

### Section B — Needs attention (only when something is wrong)
Plain sentences, each naming what happened, what it affects, and what to do. No codes, no job names.

> **1 client didn't get their order confirmation.**
> File 20020403-GLT went out without a client address on it. Someone should send it manually.
>
> **3 orders have been waiting more than 6 hours for their details.**
> They came in from SoftPro yesterday but still don't have property or party information.
>
> **TitlePoint was unreliable yesterday** — about 1 in 8 requests failed. It's working now.

When there's nothing: this section is **omitted entirely**, not shown with "Nothing requires attention."

### Section C — Yesterday in numbers (always, ~6 lines)
Business activity only. Plain nouns, no internal vocabulary.

> **Orders** — 12 came in from SoftPro, 2 opened here
> **Title reports** — 8 delivered to escrow, 3 summarised by TESSA
> **CPL documents** — 15 generated
> **Emails** — 47 sent, all delivered
> **Everything else ran on schedule** — syncs, document fetches, and email queues all healthy

That last line replaces sections 2, 5, 11 entirely: one sentence when healthy, and when not, the specific problem has already been named in Section B.

### Section D — This week (optional, small, bottom)
> Since last Thursday: **+64 orders**, **+18 agents**, **+7 escrow officers**

### Section E — Footer
> See full technical detail on the [operations dashboard](/admin/ops).

Everything cut from the email already exists there — nothing is lost, it just stops being pushed at a reader who can't use it.

### Two changes I'd pair with the rewrite
1. **Fix the delivery bug first, or the rewrite lands in an inbox half the time.** Worth understanding what began on Jul 21.
2. **Set `OPS_REPORT_RECIPIENT`** rather than relying on the hardcoded address, so the audience can change without a deploy.

### Open question for you
The report covers a rolling 8am–8am window. Plain-English framing ("yesterday") would be more honest against a **calendar day**, which means moving the send to early morning and reporting the prior midnight-to-midnight. Slightly different numbers, much easier sentence. Worth deciding before the rewrite.
